story 202
Acceptance Criteria
Account, Instrument, Order and Position are implemented and match the domain model.

A debit that would leave the balance negative is refused before anything is subtracted, and money is decimal at two places rather than a double.

A position never goes negative and an order reaches exactly one terminal state, with a disallowed transition refused by the order itself.

Tasks
Give Account the cash balance, the holder, the trading status and the only operations that move that balance, plus the answer to whether it can afford an amount.

Carry both account identifiers and keep them distinct: the numeric key is what accountId means everywhere in the API and in the JWT claim, and the string reference is what a support call quotes.

Have the account report the version it was loaded at; the domain does not take the lock.

Give Instrument the symbol in the Fauxnance scheme, the display name, the asset class, the currency of quotation and the answer to whether it may be traded.

Make delisting a flag rather than a deleted row, because the order history references the symbol and that history is the audit trail.

Implement the asymmetric average cost rule: a buy recalculates the average across the old holding and the new units, a sell reduces the quantity and leaves the average alone.

Notes
The limit price is what the customer submitted. The executed price is what the Trade Executor achieves against a live quote in Sprint 7 and does not exist until the order is filled.

story 203
Acceptance Criteria

Tasks

Notes

story 205
Acceptance Criteria
Six exception types cover the six specified cases, all descending from a single domain base type.

The base type carries the catalogue code and not an HTTP status, and the message is the catalogue message and nothing else.

Tasks
Create one type each for account not found, account not active, instrument not found, insufficient funds, insufficient holdings and duplicate order.

Put anything an investigation needs on a typed field of the exception, logged on the server, rather than in the message.

Decide what happens when a quantity or a price is out of range, given that the list of six has no member for it, and be ready to say what happens when the caller is the Trade Executor replaying an order and never ran a validator.

Declare your six exception class names in manifest.env.

Notes
One code can mean two things and one case can carry two codes, and neither is an accident. Sprint 6 maps the code to an HTTP status in one place and Sprint 7 maps it to a rejection reason on a Kafka event.

Leaking internal detail in an error body is OWASP A05.

story 206
Acceptance Criteria
Business rules 1 to 8 are implemented in the domain rather than in a controller, enforced in the stated order, and the first failure wins.

A request breaking two rules receives the code of the first: a suspended account holding no cash gets ACC-403 rather than ORD-400.

Tasks
Enforce existence, then active status, then a tradable instrument, then quantity, then price, then affordability on a buy, then held quantity on a sell, then the idempotency key.

Check quantity and price in the rules as well as on the DTO, because the domain has to hold for a caller that never ran a validator.

Leave room for the two rules that carry no code this sprint: cash and position move together or neither moves, and every order is recorded, including a rejected one.

Design a seam that makes the idempotency rule expressible and testable here without a database, since in Sprint 6 the authority is the unique constraint you built in Sprint 3.

Unit Test Execution Paths
buy succeeds and updates cash and position

buy rejected on insufficient funds

sell rejected on insufficient holdings

order rejected on an account that does not exist

order rejected on an inactive account

order rejected on an unknown or non-tradable instrument

duplicate idempotency key rejected

a request breaking two rules returns the code of the first

Notes
Rules in a controller get reimplemented by the Trade Executor, the copies drift, and the first drift accepts an order the executor then refuses.
